The reading from the Sinaxadion, for October 14th, on which we commemorate,
The Holy Martyrs Nazarios, Gerbacios, Protacios, and Kelsos.
St. Nazarios came of a noble family of great wealth, and lived at Rome in the reign of Emperor Nero
in the years 54 through 68.
The Apostle Peter brought his father, Afrikanos, and his mother, Perpetua, to believe in Christ,
and their son Nazarios, baptized by Saint Linus, whom the Apostle had appointed Bishop of Rome.
When he was 20, Nazarios left Rome, and passing through all the cities of Italy,
preaching the good news, he brought many idolaters to the knowledge of the truth, and baptized them.
He spent ten years in this way, and got to know St. Yervasios and St. Protacios while visiting the city of Piacenza,
where they were in prison on the order of the governor, Anulinos.
Their renown as Wonderworkers and fearless Apostles of Christ had spread throughout Italy.
They greeted Nazarios with a holy kiss and encouraged one another to martyr them.
But when the governor got to hear of the presence of Nazarios, he had him flagged and driven from the city.
Saint Nazarios then left for Gaul and eventually reached a city called Simeez,
where he brought a large number of the inhabitants to the faith.
The wife of one of the local dignitaries came up to him with a three-year-old child that she placed at his feet, saying,
take this child with you, let him follow you wherever you go, that he may be deemed worthy to appear beside the throne of Christ.
Nazarios accepted him with thanksgiving, baptized him with the name Celsius, and left to preach the Holy Gospel in the other cities of Gaul.
He was arrested by the governor, Daniel Baus, and to all his questions, Nazarios would only answer,
I was born a Roman, but Emma Christian, and adore the crucified one.
The governor, taking hold of the child, beat him cruelly, and Celsius wise beyond his years, cried out.
The God whom I adore will judge you, unrighteous judge, for this.
Nazarios and Celsius escaped with the help of the governor's wife, who had a terrifying vision concerning them.
They made their way to trear, preaching Christ and setting the idols at naught.
They were arrested again, brought to Rome on the emperor's orders, interrogated, and thrown into a lake.
They emerged unharmed, and went on from city to city, until they reached Milan, where they met Gervaceus and Protaceus again, as they prepared to face the final test of martyrdom.
They were all overjoyed at the prospect of meeting the Lord by partaking in his passion.
The young Celsius, who was only nine years and seven months old, was no less ready than the others to endure every torment for the love of Christ,
and the four of them walked to the place of execution with the cheerful faces of people going to a banquet.
They were beheaded, giving thanks to God for all things.
Their holy relics were discovered by St. Ambrose in the reign of Emperor Thodosius in the year 386.
Enumerable miracles were wrought through them in the city of Milan, where they can still be venerated.
Through the prayers of our Holy Father's O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us, and save us.
