The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 12th, on which we commemorate
the Holy Martyrs, Probus, Tarakos and Andronikos.
On the 12th of the month, we celebrate the memory of the Holy Martyrs, Probus, Tarakos and Andronikos.
These Holy Martyrs suffered in the reign of Diocletian about the year 304.
Tarakos, who was already well on in years, was a Roman citizen and soldier from
Claudio Polis in Isaria. Probus was from the town of Side in Pamphilia and Andronikos
came of a noble family from Ephesus.
They were discovered to be Christians at Pompeo Polis in Selissia, arrested there and
sent to appear before the governor, Numerian Maximus, first at Tarsus, next at Mopswestia,
and then for a third time in the city of Anasarbos.
Tarakos had his job broken by stones, without pity for his old age, because he was
unmoved by the governor's threats. Probus, for his part, told Maximus not to waste
time on fruitless questioning, but to put him to the torture without delay.
As he was being flogged with sinews of oxen, Maximus urged him to take pity on
himself, but he answered, this bloodshed is oil and perfume.
For me to anoint myself with joy, for further contests.
Andronikos, the youngest, likewise, expressed his eagerness to suffer for the sake of everlasting
life. They hung him on a gallows, cut his limbs with thin blades, burnt his sides, and through
salt on his wounds.
Some days later they were again brought before the governor. Tarakos was hung head down
above a brazier, billowing out thick smoke.
They poured a corrosive mixture of vinegar, mustard, and salt through his nose, before throwing
him back into prison. For his contempt of the idols and their worshipers, they sat Probus
over red-hot irons, scalped him, put burning coals on his skull, and cut out his tongue.
Andronikos was put to the test in his turn, but he never stopped mocking his tormentors.
When they forced into his mouth food and wine that had been offered to idols, Andronikos
poured scorn on the stupidity of the magistrate, who thought he had won a victory.
He made it plain that voluntary apostasy alone is a disgrace and defeat for Christians.
On the day after he had examined them for the third time, Maximus ordered games to be put
on with beasts and gladiators, intending the death of the three martyrs to be the high point
of the show. They were carried into the arena because their injuries left them unable
to walk. The beasts had already claimed a good many victims that day, but, against all
expectation, a fearsome bear went up to Andronikos and peaceably licked his wounds as if to console
him, while a lioness played harmlessly around Tarakas. The crowd was carried away in admiration
at the sight, but the governor was furious and ordered his gladiators to cut the three athletes
of Christ in pieces in the midst of the amphitheater. When night came, some pious Christians were able
with God's help to deceive the guards and went to bury the relics of the three martyrs in a mountain cave.
Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us and save us.
