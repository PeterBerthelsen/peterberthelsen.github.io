In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion, for October 11, on which we commemorate, our Holy Father
Philotheos Kokinos, patriarch of Constantinople, Saint Philotheos was born in Thessalonica towards
the end of the 13th century.
His mother was a convert from Judaism.
He was taught by the Maistros Thomas, died in the year 1347, one of the most learned men
of the time, and showed great talent for theological as well as secular studies.
But he soon left the world to embrace the monastic life.
First, at Mount Sinai, then in the monastery of the great Lavra, on Mount Athos.
He was a zealous supporter of Saint Gregory Palamas, in his valiant struggle against those
who disparaged the Hezekasts and the Orthodox theology of uncreated grace.
He was chosen to draw up the famous Hagurite Thome, the Declaration of the Monks of Mount
Athos, about the reality of the distinction between the incommunicable essence of God and
his communicable and deifying energies, whereby the saints share in that divine light,
which the three apostles saw, shining corporally in the transfiguration of the Lord on Mount
Tabor.
In the year 1340, he was appointed abbot of the monastery of Philokaloo in Thessalonica, but
was soon recalled to Mount Athos to direct the great Lavra in the year 1344.
In recognition of his contribution in the Hezekast controversy, patriarch Isador appointed
him Metropolitan of Heraclia in Thrace in the year 1347.
But, for the sake of continuing the dogmatic struggle, Philothios stayed on in Constantinople
and took part in the Hezekast Council in the year 1351 and drew up its acts.
During his absence, the city of Heraclia fell prey to the rivalry of the Genoese and
the Venetians, and it was only due to the intervention of the Holy Bishop that a large
number of the inhabitants imprisoned by the Genoese were set free.
In Matthew Catacusenus, the son of John Catacusenus came to power in the year 1354.
Patriarch Calistus, the first resigned and Philothios, renowned for his learning and his orthodoxy,
was chosen to occupy the patriarchal throne of Constantinople.
But he gave it up after only a year when John Paleologos came to power and resumed the
sea of Heraclia until he was recalled to Constantinople on the death of Calistus I in the
year 1364.
Philothios' second period as patriarch was notable for his efforts to open discussion with
the Roman Church to end the schism, not from diplomatic motives like the Emperor John
V, who had just abjured orthodoxy for the Latin faith, but out of real love for the truth.
His aim was the assembly of an ecumenical council, but the discussions came to nothing because
the Latin legates rejected his proposal.
Saint Philothios was also engaged at this time in writing a number of works, setting forth
the theology of the uncreated energies and successfully taking the issue with the humanist
theologians, who, in the works of the Western scholastics, especially Thomas Aquinas, found
a naturalistic philosophy that enabled them to express their love of classical antiquity
to the fool.
Saint Philothios also composed a large number of liturgical services, including one for St.
Gregory Palamas and compiled admirable lives of saints.
The Holy Patriarch was deposed and exiled when Andronikos IV Paleologos seized power in
the year 1376.
Born out by ill-treatment and long illness, he gave up his soul to God in the year 1379.
Through the prayers of our Holy Father's, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
