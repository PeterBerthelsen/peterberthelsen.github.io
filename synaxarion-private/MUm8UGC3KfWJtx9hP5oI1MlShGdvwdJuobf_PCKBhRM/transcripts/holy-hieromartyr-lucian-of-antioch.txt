The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 15 on which we commemorate the Holy Hieromarter
Lucian of Antioch.
On the 15th of the month, we celebrate the memory of the Holy Hieromarter Lucian, priest
of the Church of Antioch.
Antioch Lucian lived in the reign of the Emperor Diocletian in the years 284 through 304.
When his parents died, he left Antioch, the city of his birth, and gave all that he possessed
to the poor.
He made his way to Edison, where there lived a renowned master called Macarius, who instructed
him in the faith.
He was baptized and spent several years in Edison with no companions but fasting,
vigils, and tears.
His master gave him a great love for meditating upon the Holy Scriptures.
At night, as Lucian read and prayed, he was so taken up into the presence of the eternal
heavenly realities that he hardly slept at all.
The Church of Antioch, aware of his virtuous manner of life, recalled him for priestly ordination.
At Antioch, he founded the famous school of the exegits, where he guided his disciples
in the interpretation of Holy Scripture according to its literal sense.
And where, with his knowledge of Hebrew, he was able to correct the books that Heretics
had altered.
The extensive influence that Lucian exerted through his scholarly activity came to the notice
of the co-emperor Maximian, who had him arrested and brought to Nicomedia, where he was then
residing.
When he arrived there, Lucian applied himself zealously to encouraging the Christians,
many of whom, fearful of torments, were apostasizing.
He showed them from the very words of Holy Scripture that the everlasting punishments awaiting
apostates are far worse than the brief torments devised by the idolaters.
So persuasive was his word that they were all brought to repent their cowardice, and
to await with eagerness the contest of Marjardom.
For many who heard the Holy priest, it was enough to behold his radiant countenance, shining
with the grace of the Holy Spirit, to be convinced of the truth of what he said.
And so, before bringing him before his tribunal, the emperor took care to place a curtain between
himself and the saint, last he too, fall under the same spell.
As no words of his could disturb Lucian's constancy, the emperor put him to the torture and
he gave instructions that he was to be left to die of hunger and thirst in a dungeon.
As the feast of theophany drew near, disciples of Lucian from other cities came to Antioch
in large numbers to see him for the last time, and to receive his blessing.
On the day of the feast, the disciples who managed to get to his dungeon with a bread and
wine necessary for the service of the Holy Mysteries begged him once more to offer the
Holy sacrifice in their behalf.
As he had no properly consecrated altar, Saint Lucian served the divine liturgy upon his
own breast, the altar most worthy of God, who created man in his image.
The days passed and the saint seemed beyond the need of meat and drink.
When the idolaters to torment him beyond measure set a table before him, laden with food
and dishes offered to idols, he refused it with contempt, and each time they suggested he
give in exclaimed, I am a Christian.
On the third occasion of giving this answer, he meekly gave up his soul to God.
His body was cast into the sea at the Emperor's command, but Adolphin carried it on his back
to the shore, so the faithful were able to give it honorable burial and partake of the grace
that flowed from his holy relics.
Under the prayers of our Holy Father's, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
