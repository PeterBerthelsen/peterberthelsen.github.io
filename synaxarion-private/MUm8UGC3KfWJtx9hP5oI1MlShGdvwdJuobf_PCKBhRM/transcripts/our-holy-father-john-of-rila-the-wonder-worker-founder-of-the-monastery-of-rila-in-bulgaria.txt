In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 19, on which we commemorate, our Holy Father
John of Rila, the Wonderworker, founder of the monastery of Rila in Bulgaria.
Our Holy Father John came of a devout family that lived not far from the city of Sophia
in the reign of Peter I, King of the Bulgars, in the year 927 through 968.
And of Constantine the Seventh Porphyrogenitos, in the years 913 through 959.
From an early age, he led a remarkably virtuous life, and as soon as he could, gave away
all his goods to the poor, and withdrew to a monastery to engage in the labors of the
ascetic life.
Following a vision, he left for a mountainous area to live alone with the One God, intent
upon nothing but prayer.
His nephew, Luke, though still a child, was likewise consumed by an eager desire for
God, and came to join his uncle in the wilderness to lead the angelic life under his direction.
But the boy's father was very angry, and came to take him away by force from John, whom
he accused of taking advantage of the lad's youth.
The saint may well have reminded him of the Word of the Lord, let the little children come
to me, and do not hinder them, for to such belongs the kingdom of heaven.
But there was nothing for it, and Luke had to return weeping to the world.
But by the prayer of the saint, God did not allow the child's innocence to be stained by
the taint of worldliness, and at the same time showed his father the futility of opposing
the divine will, for within a very short time the child was taken away by illness to the
kingdom of heaven.
The saint was attacked by brigands, blind instruments of demons, and had to leave his solitude
and look for a refuge in the grim Rila mountains.
He settled in an inaccessible cave at the summit of an outcrop of rock, finding shelter
there from the world and its empty show.
King Peter, however, heard of him and sent to seek him out, but John would not leave his
desert even for the king.
On hearing how matters stood, the king decided that he would visit the saint himself, but he
was only able to see the cave from a distance, so he sent John, gold, and provisions, and
a letter full of humility in which he asked for the saint's prayers to enable him to triumph
over the temptations of pleasure and of the vain glory that a sale all who, like himself,
are accounted great in the world.
John returned the gold, saying that he had no more need of it, nor of any of the treasures
that men amass in this world of vanity.
He wrote to the king, counseling him to join repentance, tears, and the constant remembrance
of death to the royal virtues of compassion and mercy, so that seeing his humility, the king
of kings might grant him possession of the kingdom of heaven.
When he received this letter, the king kissed it with veneration as his most precious treasure.
He often read it for consolation in the cares of state.
John for his part remained in the cave of Rila until the end of his life.
As a great many of his disciples entreated him to let them live beside him, he built a church
nearby, and then monastic cells which soon grew into the majestic monastery that to this day
holds sway over the mountains of Rila and contains the relics of the saint.
Even under the Turkish occupation, the monastery continued to be the spiritual center of the Bulgarian
people and the main source of their ecclesiastical culture.
Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us and save us.
