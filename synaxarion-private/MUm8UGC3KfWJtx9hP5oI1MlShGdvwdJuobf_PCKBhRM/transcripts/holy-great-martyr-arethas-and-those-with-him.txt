In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion, for October 24th, on which we commemorate, the holy
and great martyr Aretha's and those with him.
On the 24th of the month, we celebrate the memory of the holy great martyr Aretha's and
his companions.
The Holy King Elisban reigned at Oksum over the kingdom of Ethiopia in the days of Justin,
the pious emperor of the Romans in the years 518 through 527.
Ethiopia's nearest neighbor, the kingdom of Himyar, in Arabia Felix, was in the hands
of Dunuas, a cruel and war-hungry tyrant who converted to Judaism under the name of Yusuf
and was the sworn enemy of the Christian kingdom of Ethiopia.
Elisban invaded his territory in the year 518, brought him to his knees in a series of brilliant
victories and exacted a tribute.
And after a while, Dunuas got together a formidable army and attacked the Christian cities
of his kingdom, meaning to wipe out all who would not deny Christ and trample on the holy
and life-giving cross.
He made for the city of Narjan, in North Yemen, a rich and populous place that had been
Christian since the reign of Constantius in the years 337 through 360.
The son of Saint Constantine the Great.
The governor of the city and province was Arethas, his head and hairs white as snow, whom
everyone looked upon with veneration by reason of his wisdom in virtue.
Dunuas disposed his 12,000 men for the siege and started to provoke the defenders from beneath
the ramparts with a thousand threats, swearing to put them all to the sword unless they surrender
and renounce their faith.
But he was disappointed to see that his threats served only to make the Christians more willing
to shed their blood for Christ.
He began to fear heavy losses from a siege and decided to change his tactics to trickery.
On the strength of soft words and lying promises, the scoundrel managed to persuade the chief
citizens to let him into the city, with a small retinue for a ceremonial entry as sovereign
of the province.
They opened their gates to him, full of confidence in his promises, and in the protection of
God.
Smiling with a kindly condescension unusual in him, Dunuas was full of praise for the city,
its buildings, and amenities, its prosperity, and the harmony that reigned among the inhabitants.
As he took his leave, he invited the leaders of the people and the dignitaries to visit
him in his camp the following day.
One on the Morrow, the gates were opened to allow the chief citizens led by St. Arithas
to go forth.
Dunuas ordered them all to be seized.
The consternation and confusion of the people at that moment was the opportunity for his soldiers
to get into the city, which they took and sacked.
The tyrant first wrecked his wrath on the Holy Bishop Paul, who had died two years before.
He had his tomb opened and ordered his holy and much venerated relics to be burnt.
He went on to burn all the priests, monks, nuns, and clergy in the city to the number of
477 persons.
He beheaded 127 pious layfolk who offered their life to Christ.
He had a rich widow, a noble woman, brought before him.
When persuasion failed of its purpose, he threatened dreadful tortures if she would not deny
the faith.
Her daughter, seeing her mother insulted by the tyrant and ill treated by his soldiers, ran
at Dunuas and spat in his face.
He was enraged and had the twelve-year-old girl immediately beheaded.
Then in the height of cruelty he made the mother drink a cup of her daughter's blood, before
be heading her in her turn.
The next day, high on his throne, the tyrant had St. Arithas and his 340 companions brought
before him.
Arithas had to be carried to the place of judgment for so distressed was he at seeing his old age
such torments raining down upon his fellow citizens, that his strength had left him.
But still, his confidence and courage before the king showed the metal of a young warrior.
Meekly and calmly, he encouraged his companions to reach perfection by way of martyrdom, and
to be of good cheer in offering themselves to share in the Savior's Passion, so that
they might partake of the everlasting joy of his glory.
The people went copiously as they listened to his exhortation and assured him with one voice,
that the bond of charity that held them together in this transitory life would remain unbroken
until death, and that they were all ready to receive the crown of martyrdom with him.
Faced with their steadfastness, the king abandoned any attempt to make them deny their faith,
and gave them the order to behead them near the river.
After a final prayer, the martyrs saluted one another with a holy kiss, like priests preparing
to offer the holy sacrifice, and Arethas was beheaded first of all.
The others devoutly anointed their foreheads with the saints' blood, and met their death
with joy.
Thereupon a woman with a three-year-old boy approached the place of martyrdom, and likewise
anointed herself with some drops of the martyr's blood.
The soldiers seized her and brought her to the tyrant who straightaway ordered them to
burn her alive.
The child cried pitifully, as a fledgling does that loses its mother.
The little boy's appearance in manner pleased the king, and he took him on his lap, and tried
to comfort him.
He asked the child to tell him what he wanted most of all, and was very surprised to hear
the little ones say that he wanted to be a martyr, like his mother.
But what does being a martyr mean, he asked him?
It means dying for Christ, so as to come alive again, the infant replied.
But have you any idea who this Christ is?
Come to church, I'll show you the child answered confidently.
The boy insisted on this, and thereby showed himself wiser than the aged of this world.
When he saw them throwing his mother into the fire, he suddenly freed himself from the
king's embrace, ran up to the fire, and, without hesitation, plunged into the flames to be
united with her in Christ.
The notoriety of the massacre at Narjan was such that it came to the notice of the Emperor
Justin at Constantinople.
He wrote to Asterius the patriarch of Alexandria asking him to urge Elisban, the king of Ethiopia,
to mount a punitive expedition against the infamous Du Nuas.
Asterius put the matter to the monks of Naitria and of other deserts who kept vigils of intercession,
and fasted for the success of the expedition and the liberation of the Christians.
The great Elisban was doubtful of success, and desiring a sign from God, went to ask a
hermit of renown in his kingdom for his prayers and counsel.
The holy man assured him that by the prayers and tears of the Emperor Justin, of the patriarch
Asterius and his monks, and of himself also, God would so certainly deliver the shameless
tyrant into his hands, that he adjured him to take no more than twenty days' supplies for
his troops.
The Christian army fought valiantly, and with God's help quickly rewound the city of Narjan,
and the territory of Himyar in the year 525.
Elisban was the minister of the wrath of God upon all the enemies of the Christians.
He established a bishop in the city, built new churches with the fortune left by Saint
Arithas, and then returned in peace to his kingdom.
Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ, our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
