In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
The reading from the Sinaxarion for October 20th, on which we commemorate, our Holy Father
Jonah, Bishop of Manchuria, St. Jonah Bokrovsky was born at Caluga in the year 1888 and named
Valodia in Holy Baptism. He was orphan at the age of eight years and brought up by a village
Deacon who sent him to the seminary in his native town. From there, he went to the theological
academy in Kazan. In his third year, he was transferred a monk at Optina monastery.
A brilliant student, on graduating, he was asked to teach the New Testament course at the academy,
a position he reluctantly accepted in obedience to his spiritual Father, the righteous elder
Gabriel. He was later ordained priest and continued to teach at the academy until his 30th year,
when the Bolsheviks seized power in the year 1918 and he was forced to leave Kazan. He was
arrested in Perm and beaten up so badly that he lost consciousness. He managed to escape the
Omsk and joined the White Southern Volunteers Army as Senior Chaplain. He shared the tribulations
of this company in its retreat through the mountains of Turkistan and the Gobi Desert into China,
where many Russians had found refuge. Soon after being received into the ecclesiastical mission at
Bekeeng, the young Hyromonc was consecrated Bishop of Manchuria. At the time of his arrival in the
autumn of 1922, the border town of Manchuria, today Manchuli, was full of Russian refugees,
who possessed scarcely more than the clothes they stood up in. The native population helped as much
as it could, but its resources were inadequate to meet the needs at hand, and there was not
bread enough, even for the children. During the three short years of his episcopate, St. Jonah
was an inspiration to his flock, whose life was transformed through the activity of the man of prayer
and the apostle of love that was their bishop. Each word of the divine liturgy, as he served it,
penetrated hearts. His sermons too were heartfelt. Each one was different and possessed such
inspiration that no one wanted to miss a single word. His overriding concern was the commandment of
love for the neighbor. He fed the hungry, gave drink to the thirsty, took in the stranger, clothed
the naked and visited the sick, affirmed archbishop Militius of Harbin. St. Jonah's care of his flock
extended to organizing provision for the practical necessities of life in exile.
He saw to repair and upkeep the church buildings and everything needed for worship.
He opened a library, organized a soup kitchen, and established a clinic and a grammar school,
staffed by qualified doctors and teachers from among the refugees. The children loved him even more
than their own parents, and when he talked to them in divinity lessons, they were all captivated by
his words, and were able to repeat exactly what he has said to them. When it came to collecting funds
for his charitable works, Bishop Jonah showed remarkable business acumen. He seemed capable of
transforming every languishing enterprise into a flourishing one whose profits would go towards his
main aim of feeding and looking after the children. Having lost his own parents in early youth,
the holy bishop well knew the needs of poor and uprooted young people.
Overcoming every difficulty by the power of faith and love, he founded an orphanage that was
providing shelter for 28 children by the end of its first year. Never relinquishing his monastic
asceses, St. Jonah gave of himself unstintedly in all that he did. After taking care of a priest who
died of typhoid fever, he contracted chronic tonsillitis but continued in selfless service of his flock,
without giving much thought to his health. One day he rinsed his throat with kerosene,
supposing it would act as an antiseptic, but it rapidly led to blood poisoning.
When it became evident that death was near at hand, the saint began preparing himself solemnly and
imperturbably for his passage to eternity. In his testament of which several thousand copies were
distributed on the day of his funeral, he wrote, I began here with the words of the apostle of love,
children, love one another, and I am ending with these same words, love one another.
This is your bishops' commandment. Do not abandon the children. Forgive me for the sake of Christ.
Do not forget me in your holy prayers. And so until eternity, when we shall all stand
at the dread judgment, having bid farewell and blessed those crowded tearfully in his quarters,
he put on the epitrahelion and cuffs that had belonged to older ambros of optina,
and began loudly with prostrations to read the canon for the departure of the soul.
Then he laid on his bed and said, God's will be done. Now I shall die.
Within minutes his soul was transported to that other world which knows neither sickness nor sorrow,
nor sighing but the life everlasting in the joy of the Lord.
His funeral was accompanied by the universal mourning of his flock and the holy bishop appeared
that night to a paralyzed ten-year-old boy who was miraculously healed. Through the prayers of our
holy fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us and save us.
