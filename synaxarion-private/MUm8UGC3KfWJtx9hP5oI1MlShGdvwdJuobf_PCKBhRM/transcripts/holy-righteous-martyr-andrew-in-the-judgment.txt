In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 17th, on which we commemorate the Holy
Rites' martyr Andrew in the judgment, Saint Andrew came from the island of Crete and lived
in the reign of the iconoclast emperor Constantine V Copronimos.
In the years 741 through 775, from early childhood he labored with all his might to acquire the
virtues and became a monk.
When he heard of the persecutions that Orthodox Christians were suffering, at the hands of
those who hated the holy icons, he decided to go to Constantinople to confess the true
faith.
When he got there, he went to the palace named after the great martyr Mamas, where the
emperor was engaged in interrogating the holy and venerable ascetics accused of worshipping
the holy icons.
The saint was deeply distressed at this spectacle.
He fearlessly went up to Copronimos and asked, emperor, are you really a Christian?
The tyrant was, for a moment, taken aback by the audacity of this unknown monk, but then
ordered his guards to seize him for his intolerable insolence.
They pounded on Andrew like ferocious animals, threw him to the ground, beat him with sinews
of oxen, and hammered his face with stones to silence his confession of the true faith.
But nothing could vanquish the saint's zeal.
With his mouth all bleeding, he addressed the emperor, saying,
If you, an earthly king, punish, as for an offense against your own person, anyone daring
to deface statues or pictures of yourself, how much more have you to fear God's wrath
and chastisement, for insulting and destroying the icons of our Lord Jesus Christ and of his
saints?
These words infuriated the emperor.
He ordered them to tie the saint to a rope and drag him to the marketplace.
At the sight of his blood, reddening the ground, one of the bystanders was driven by the evil
one to throw himself on the valiant athlete of Christ, and cut off one of his legs with
a butcher's knife.
Under this final torment, Saint Andrew gave up his soul to God.
They took his holy body to a place called crisis, the judgment, where the corpses of criminals
were thrown.
Some while later, twelve men possessed by evil spirits went to the place all at the same
time, but by no previous arrangement.
Among the bodies, they found the relics of the saint, which they took away and buried
in a holy place, receiving in turn deliverance from their afflictions.
Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
