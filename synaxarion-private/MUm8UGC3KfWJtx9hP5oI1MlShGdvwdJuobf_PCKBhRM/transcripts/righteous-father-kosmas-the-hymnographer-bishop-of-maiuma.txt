In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
Though reading, from the Sinaxadion, for October 14, on which we commemorate, our righteous
Father Cosmos, the himnographer, Bishop of Mayuma, St. Cosmos was left in orphan when he
was still very young.
He was adopted by Sirgeus, the father of St. John of Damascus, and a man of high rank and
great wealth in that city.
Sirgeus saw to it that his own son, John, and his adopted son Cosmos, received an excellent
education at the hands of a Sicilian monk, himself called Cosmos, who taught them all he knew.
Both young men were very able students, and it was not long before everyone was amazed
at their knowledge of grammar, philosophy, music, astronomy, and geometry.
They both became monks in the Lava of St. Saba's, and so concluded their study of earthly wisdom
by applying themselves to the science of sciences, Askees and prayer, which is the true philosophy
After John became a priest, the members of the Synod of Jerusalem prevailed upon Cosmos to accept
the bishopric of Mayuma, a church dependent on Jerusalem.
He tended his spiritual flock in peace for many years and fell asleep in the Lord.
He is well known as the composer of many poetic canons, mainly for the great feasts of the
Lord.
The Holy Spirit gave him the ability to express the essence of the theological teaching of
the fathers of the church in the form of hymns, thereby making their doctrine accessible to
all the faithful as they come together in the churches to sing the glory of God.
Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
