The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 13, on which we commemorate, the Holy Martyrs
Carposs, Papilos, Agatha Doros, and Agathonica at Pergamos.
On the 13th of the month, we celebrate the memory of the Holy Martyrs Carposs Papilos
Agatha Doros and Agathonica.
These Holy Martyrs suffered about the year 250 in the time of Emperor Desias when Valerian
was Pro-Consul of the East.
Carposs was the son of a priest of the idols.
He believed in Christ, was baptized, and eventually became Bishop of the Church in Pergamon,
established by Saint John the Theologian.
Papilos came from Thyatira.
He was instructed in the faith and baptized by Carposs, who's deacon he became at Pergamon,
where he spread the word of God.
They were both arrested and brought before the Pro-Consul Valerian because they failed to
obey the imperial edict, ordering the surrender of the sacred vessels and vestments to the
authorities.
They fearlessly confessed the Lord and declared themselves ready to endure any torment,
rather than deny Christ by worshiping senseless idols.
In his anger, the Pro-Consul had them harnessed behind his horses and made to run a distance
of some forty miles in front of his chariot, all the way from Thyatira to Sardis.
When they got to Sardis, they were tied to a trussle and flayed.
Agatha Doros, one of Carposs's servants, followed after them.
An angel gave him confidence that it was time he too confessed Christ God with his blood.
So he went up to the tormentors and professed his faith.
They straightway seized him, stretched him on the trussle with the two other martyrs
and beat him to death.
Hanging from the trussle and put to all kinds of torture, Carposs smiled.
The Pro-Consul was amazed and asked him why.
I have seen the glory of the Lord, he answered and I am rejoicing.
They tied Papilos to four long stakes and stoned him, but God protected him in this trial,
and he and Carposs were again brought before the Pro-Consul.
Then they were dragged on their backs through brambles, beaten on the stomach, and in that
state thrown to the beasts in the arena.
Then an astonishing thing happened, a lion taking on a man's voice, up-brated the persecutors
with their cruelty to Christ's holy martyrs.
But the pagans stopped their ears, unwilling to recognize what even brute beasts acknowledged.
They nailed iron sandals onto the saints' feet and threw them into a blazing furnace.
As the crackling frames devoured him, Carposs cried out, blessed be thou, Lord Jesus Christ,
son of God, who has dain't to give me the sinner, a share in thine inheritance.
At that moment a woman's voice was heard.
I too, she cried, have seen the glorious feast, and I wish to sit down and partake of it.
Her relatives vainly tried to restrain Agathonica, who some say was Papilose's sister, and reminded
her of her duties towards her child, but she, accounting as nothing the natural weakness
of her sex, rushed into the flames crying, Lord, I flee unto thee, save me.
Then she expired, and her soul departed to the heavenly mansions to rejoice with her
companions in the refreshment of the dew of heaven.
Through the prayers of our Holy Father's O Lord, Jesus Christ, our God, have mercy on us,
and save us.
