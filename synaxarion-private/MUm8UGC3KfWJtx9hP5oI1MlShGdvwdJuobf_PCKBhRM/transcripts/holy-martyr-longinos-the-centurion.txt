In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
The reading from the Sinoxadion for October 16, on which we commemorate, the holy martyr
Langenas, the Centurion.
Saint Langenas lived in the reign of the Emperor Tiberius in the years 15 through 34 AD.
He came from Cappadocia and served as a Centurion in the Roman army, under the orders of Pilate,
the governor of Judea.
He and his men were commanded to carry into effect Pilate's sentence regarding the holy
passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, and to guard the tomb for fear the disciples should come
and steal away his body to lend credence to his resurrection.
So it came about that Langenas witnessed all the astonishing miracles that accomplished
Christ's passion, the earthquake, the darkening of the sun, the rending of the veil of the
temple, the splitting of the rocks, the opening of the graves, and the appearance in their
risen bodies of many of the saints of ancient times.
The eyes of the Centurion's heart were opened at the sight of these portents, and he cried
with a loud voice, truly, this man was the Son of God.
On the third day when the guards witnessed the angel appearing to the holy women at the
tomb, they shook with fear and became as dead men.
Some of them went to tell the chief priests of the Jews all that had happened.
The chief priests and elders met and decided to give Langenas and his men a large sum of money
if they would put it about that the disciples had come by night and stolen away Christ's
body while the guards were asleep.
But Langenas and two of his soldiers refused this money, for they were now and forever
illumined by the light of faith in the resurrection.
So leaving the army and his military duties behind him, Langenas went back to his home country
of Cappadocia, where he preached the good news just as the apostles were doing.
When Pilate learned of this, he wrote to the Emperor Tiberius denouncing Langenas, which
the chief priests bent on vengeance had prevailed on him to do by giving him money and presence.
The men, the Emperor, sent in search of Langenas called, in God's Providence, at the house
where he had taken refuge.
They asked for hospitality and for information as to the whereabouts of the deserter who was
unknown to them by sight.
It was the saint himself who received them with the care for strangers' customary among
disciples of Christ.
As they talked, Langenas was exceedingly joyful on learning the purpose of their journey,
and he redoubled his efforts to put his guests at their ease.
Then he left them, and with complete serenity prepared his grave and everything necessary
for his burial.
He went to look for the two companions who had fled from Palestine with him, and they
decided to offer themselves to martyrdom at his side.
He then went back to his guests, and told them that he was the Langenas they were looking
for to put to death.
The Emperor's agents were astounded at the calmness of his manner, and considering the hospitality
they have received, deeply distressed because of the dark deed they had come to do.
But the saint implored them to make haste in uniting him and his companions to their Lord
and Master.
Sick at heart, they beheaded the three disciples of Christ, and sent the head of Saint Langenas
to Jerusalem to certify Pilate and the chief priests that he had indeed been put to
the death.
His head was then thrown into a cesspit outside the city.
Many years later a wealthy capidotian lady, who had fallen ill and lost her sight, came
on pilgrimage to Jerusalem to pray for healing accompanied by her only son.
But upon the arrival in the holy city her son died suddenly, adding yet a greater sorrow
to the anguish of the unfortunate lady.
One night Saint Langenas appeared to her in a dream and revealed the spot where his head
was buried, with the assurance that she would receive healing from his precious relic.
After an asidious search, the pious woman found the holy martyr's head, and her blindness
was indeed taken away by the divine grace resting upon the relic.
Not only were her bodily eyes opened, but God granted her to see with the eyes of her soul
that her son had his place beside Saint Langenas in the abode of the blessed, comforted and
full of gratitude to God, who knows how to render a hundredfold to those whom he chasens.
She placed the relic of the holy martyr and the body of her son in a ferretary, that
she took back to capidotia and placed in a church that she built in the saint's honor.
Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
