In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 19th, on which we commemorate the holy martyr
Vados and his companions.
The memory of the holy martyr Vados and of his companions, the six ascetics who were slain
by the sword, and also of the venerable Cleopatra and her son, John.
Saint Vados came of a devout Christian family of noble origin.
He was an army officer serving in Egypt in the reign of Maximian circa the year 304.
His courage and integrity won him the emperor's favor, but this honor could not prevent him
looking with wonder and longing on the zeal of the holy martyrs.
He used to visit seven holy ascetics in the cell where imprisoned for the faith they
were awaiting trial.
In the day junior and one of them died, Vados took this as a providential sign of the way
to overcome his fear of offering himself for martyrdom alone.
He made up his mind to take his stand before the judge alongside the other martyrs in
place of him who had died.
When he went forward and said who he was, the judge could not conceal his surprise.
But his anger flared up unrestrained.
When the saint made it clear that nothing was going to make him change his mind about
preferring the everlasting delights promised to faithful soldiers of the Lord above the pleasures
of this world.
The judge had him hung from a trussle and ordered them to flog him with heavy scourges to the
breaking of his bones.
The saint looked steadfastly at the six ascetics who were to witness this sight and ask them
to pray to God he finds strength in the ordeal.
For the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.
No sooner had they raised their hands to heaven and addressed fervent prayer to the Lord
than Vados felt as it were, a hand supporting him and warding off the blows before they reached
him.
Covered as he was by the grace of the Holy Spirit, he bore mistreatment joyfully.
For the sharpness of the torture was completely taken away.
His untroubled confidence increased the rage of his tormentors tenfold.
They fixed him to the earth and clawed his sides so deeply that his bowels spilt upon
the ground, and then he was hung up again.
He gave up his soul to God after five hours, during which he found the strength to utter
words of encouragement to his companions.
Among the bystanders at the Marjoram of St. Vados was a devout woman from Palestine called Cleopatra.
At nightfall she returned with her young son, John, and some other Christians, and took
up the precious relics of the Marjoram and buried them beneath her dwelling.
When she returned to her own country some years later, she took the relics with her, making
out that they were her deceased husband's remains.
She enshrined them in a place near Mount Taber that was soon frequented by many of the faithful
who sought the healing of their ills from the saint.
A church was built there, and on the day of its consecration after Cleopatra and John
had worked hard all day serving the pilgrims, the lad suddenly fell ill and died.
His mother immediately placed his body on the saint's tomb, and bathed in tears she begged
him to act in her behalf for the sake of all she had done for him.
Utterly exhausted, she fell asleep there, and saw St. Vados in a dream.
She was holding her son in his arms, and both of them were clothed in shining garments.
The saint reproached her for her lack of faith, and assured her that he had taken her son
under his protection.
The child, for his part, left her in no doubt that he had no wish to give up the life
of eternal blessedness in order to return to this world of sorrow.
Uncleopatra asked to join them, St. Vados advised her to work for a while longer at her salvation,
and blessing her they vanished from sight.
When she awoke, the devout woman dressed the body of her son in festal raiment, and full
of joy proceeded with his funeral.
She distributed all her goods to the poor, and devoted herself to the service of the
martyr's sanctuary.
Her fasts, vigils, alms, and prayers were so well pleasing to God that she was accounted
worthy of contemplating her son in his bright dwelling place every week on the Lord's
day.
After seven years, she gave up her soul in peace to God, and went to join St. Vados and
her son in the glory of the elect.
Through the prayers of our Holy Father's, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us,
and save us.
