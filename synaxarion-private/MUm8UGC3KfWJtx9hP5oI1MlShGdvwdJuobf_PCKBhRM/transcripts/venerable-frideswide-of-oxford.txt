In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen!
The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 19th, on which we commemorate, the venerable
Frideswide of Oxford, the memory of the Holy Abyss Frideswide of Oxford.
St. Frideswide was a princess of West Saxon lineage, seeing that she desired above all to dedicate
her virginity to the king of heaven and to dwell in the house of the Lord forever.
Her father, King Deyada, founded a monastery for her in his territory at Oxford near the
River Thames. There she directed a double community of nuns and monks, as St. Hilda did at Whitby,
but a prince from another kingdom, incited by the evil one, became enamored of the Lord's handmaid
and troubled the peace of her monastic profession for a long time, until he was struck, blind,
to rebuke his contempt. St. Frideswide was strict in her askecies. She gave all she had to the poor,
for what things were gained to her, those she counted, lost for Christ. She wore a hair shirt,
and ate only a little barley bread with a few vegetables and drank nothing but water.
She ruled her monastery in all wisdom for many years, and fell asleep in the Lord about the year 735.
Later St. Frideswides became the mother church of Oxford University, where scholars solemnly visited
her shrine twice a year to ask for her heavenly protection. In the 16th century, the shrine was
defaced, and her holy relics were dishonored, but they still lie at Christ's church in Oxford.
Through the prayers of our Holy Father's, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us and save us.
