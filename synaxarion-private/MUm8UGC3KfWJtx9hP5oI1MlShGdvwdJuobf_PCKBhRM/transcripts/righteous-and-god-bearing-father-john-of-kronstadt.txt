In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion for October 19, on which we commemorate our righteous and
God-bearing Father, John of Christad, John Elig, Sergiev, was born on the 19th of October
in the year 1829 into a poor family from Surah, a small village in the province of Archangel
and the far north of Russia.
His father, who was a sacristan, brought him up with love for the church and its services
and for prayer, and he encouraged him to seek refuge and consolation, intribulations in
God alone.
When a schoolboy, he had great difficulty in learning until God removed the heaviness from
his spirit in one night, in response to his fervent prayers, and he became an outstanding
pupil, winning a scholarship to continue his studies at the Theological Academy of St. Petersburg.
As a student, he was interested in every subject, and he read widely, but above all, he loved
prayer and giving glory to God while walking alone in the countryside.
On the death of his father, he had to work as a secretary in addition to his studies in order
to help support the family.
Burdened by poverty and with temptations to discouragement, he struggled in urgent and
persistent prayer in ploying God to grant him the grace of faith and joy.
Sadness, he would say, is apostasy and death of the heart.
He regarded everything that happened in his life as a sign from God, which is why he
gave up his notion of missions to far away China in order to become a missionary in his own
land.
Following a dream, he agreed to marry the daughter of the Archpriest of the Cathedral
of Kronstadt, the naval base near St. Petersburg, where all the poverty, social injustice and moral
degradation of a society on the brink of catastrophe were concentrated.
On the day of his marriage, John said to his wife,
Lisa, there are enough happy families.
Let us, you and I, put ourselves at the service of God.
And indeed, they remained in perfect chastity until the end of their days, addressing each
other as brother and sister.
Or dained on the 12th of November in the year 1855, Father John based his ministry on constant
meditation of the Holy Scriptures, nightly vigil devoted to the Jesus Prayer.
And above all, daily celebration of the Holy Liturgy.
The divine liturgy, he wrote in his journal, My Life in Christ, is truly the service of heaven
upon earth, during which God himself, in a special way, immediate and very close, is present
and dwells with men, being himself the invisible celebrant who offers and is offered.
There is nothing greater, holier, more sublime, more life-giving than the liturgy.
For him, all the activities of the priest, including the pastoral care of his flock, were
an extension of the liturgical mystery of the priesthood of Christ, who accomplishes
the salvation and sanctification of mankind in the church.
As the priest is the living image of Christ, Saint John made it his task from the outset
of his ministry to carry the shining and life-giving presence of the friend of man into the most
wretched and least slubrious districts.
He would go into houses, taking the children in his arms, and the parents would be converted
by his words, Redalent of a gentleness and affection to which they were not accustomed.
He tended to the sick, transforming their bed of pain into a bed of good cheer through
the consolation of the faith.
He gave alms of everything he possessed and would often return home with neither shoes
nor coat.
He would go to every place, not to judge, but to pray, and to bring the presence of Christ
wherever he came.
Pushing through the crowds thronging his way, his spirit fixed constantly in God.
He spread the sweet savor of Christ and his loving kindness as the sun sheds its rays.
Some he would bless, for others he would pray according to their requests, and with his
left hand he would immediately distribute the gifts that he received with his right.
It was not long before his manner of life attracted criticisms and slanders from all sides.
He was accused of being out of his mind, but he continued his work nevertheless, rejoicing
to be held up to ridicule for the love of Christ.
In spite of innumerable difficulties he succeeded thanks to the increasing number of gifts he received
in founding the House of Labor, a vast charitable institution that included a church, schools,
asylums and workshops, with thousands of poverty-stricken folk not only received material assistance
but also regained their dignity through education and participation in the life of the church.
For 32 years St. John added the teaching of schoolchildren to the sum of his pastoral activities.
He set education of the heart above the accumulation of knowledge, and he prepared the children
to receive the grace of God by giving them a sense of the beauty of the universe and
of respect for each person as made in the image of God.
As the years passed, St. John's love for mankind grew in proportion to the spread of his
renown beyond the town of Kronstadt.
The priest should have compassion for the whole world, he said.
He should be everything for everyone.
In return God gave his prayer extraordinary power to heal the body, to convert and to console
afflicted souls, showing him thereby that his mission was to be a living column of prayer
and of intercession for the whole people to become the pastor of all Russia.
In the latter part of his life there was a change of direction in his communion with mankind.
It was no longer so much himself going forth to seek out the afflicted, as the Christ-loving
people of Russia hastening to come to him.
They arrived in Kronstadt every day by the thousand to receive counsel and assistance to
ask for his prayers or just to see him.
The post office had to run a special service to deliver the mass of letters, telegrams and
money orders that arrived for him daily.
With this money St. John provided for more than a thousand poor people and founded a good
number of churches and monasteries.
He used to get up at 3 a.m. and make his way to the already crowded church for matins.
At the time of the Proceses, the Prosphora, offer Tory bread, would be carried up in huge
baskets, together with endless lists of names.
The saint would take baskets in his hand and send up an ardent prayer to God, as if he
were interceding for each person in particular.
His celebration of the divine liturgy was wonderful to behold, standing before the altar, as
before the throne of the God of glory.
He would address him in accents which moved the hardest of hearts, and he never communicated
without his face streaming with tears.
When I do not serve the liturgy, I die, he used to say.
In his sermons he would exhort the Christians to communicate frequently, for at that time,
it was often regarded as sufficient to take communion only once a year.
Since he could not hear the confession of each person, the faithful of their own accord
recovered the ancient practice of public confession.
Penitents confessed with tears all their sins in the presence of their brethren, before going
to draw the water of new life at the wellspring of joy.
By his words and equally, in all that he did, Saint John received the grace of communicating
his extraordinary awareness of the presence of Christ.
Jesus is my breath, even more than the air, at every moment of my life.
He is my light before every other light, my food and my drink, my clothing, my savor, my
sweetness, my father and my mother, a ground solider than the earth which cannot be shaken,
to which carries me.
After the liturgy which ended about noon, he would spend the rest of the day receiving requests
for prayers, visiting his charitable works, inspiring faith, hope and joy in those in the depths
of despair, and he would not return home until late at night.
In spite of these many fold activities, his spirit never turned aside from prayer, for having
become God by grace, his actions and his words were prayers charged with divine energy.
Towards the end of his life he was cruelly tried by illness, but he bore it all with gentleness,
patience and thanksgiving.
Having predicted the day of his death, he fell asleep in the Lord on the 20th of December
in the year 1908, surrounded by the veneration of the whole Russian people from the poorest
of the poor to the imperial family.
Sent by God as a prophet, Saint John of Crunchdent was at the origin of the spiritual awakening
of the Russian people, on the eve of the revolution, and a steward of the loving kindness
of God among men, he has shown what it is truly to be an orthodox priest.
Through the prayers of our Holy Father's, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
