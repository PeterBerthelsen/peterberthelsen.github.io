In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion, for October 22, on which we commemorate our Holy Father
Averkios, the Wonder Worker.
On the 22nd of the month, we celebrate the memory of our Holy Father, the equal to the
Apostles and Wonder Worker Averkios, Bishop of Yerapolis.
Saint Averkios was Bishop of Yerapolis, Infrigia, Salutaris, in the west of Asia Minor.
During the reign of the Emperor Marcus Aurelius in the years 161-180, who, although reputed
a philosopher, was a fierce persecutor of Christians, he ordered all his subjects to join in great
celebrations which were to be held all over the Empire in honor of the idols, so as to discover
the Christians who dared withstand the imperial religion.
While the inhabitants of Yerapolis were dutifully offering their sacrifices and indulging in
the dissipation, usual to those demonic festivities, Averkios went into his house alone
and prayed with tears that the Lord would have mercy on the ignorant people.
Thereupon he saw an angel in a vision who commanded him to throw down the alters of Apollo
and of the pagan gods.
Strong in the Lord and in the power of his might, Averkios immediately got up and went out
into the night to cast down all the dumb idols.
Next morning, on seeing a sight that vividly showed up the emptiness of their religion,
the inhabitants were minded to rush on his house and put him to death.
But far from fleeing, the Bishop went to the marketplace to confess the true faith openly.
The anger of the crowd rose at his audacity, but on seeing him use the very staff with which
he had thrown down the idols to restore three possessed men to their right mind.
The people calmed down and listened in fear and amazement to a man endowed with such great
power.
One hundred of them believed with all their soul in Christ God and were baptized that day.
After that, not only the inhabitants of the city, but people from all over that region
and from neighboring countries hastened to the saint for the sake of his teaching and for
the cure of their diseases.
The miracles that Averkios brought were so many and so striking that even Marcus Averkios
got to hear of them and sent for him to come to Rome because his betrothed daughter was
tormented by an unclean spirit. During the whole course of his journey, Averkios spread around
him a host of blessings by his miracles and the might of his inspired word. When he arrived in Rome,
he was immediately taken to the Imperial Palace where the Empress Faustina was anxiously awaiting him.
She brought him to her daughter who was thrown into violent convulsions when she saw the saint.
Using her voice, the demon begged the servant of Jesus Christ to torment it no longer,
but to let it return to Frigia once it came. Averkios allowed this but ordered it to take away a massive
stone altar used for idolatrous rites in Rome. The crowd that stood by was amazed to see the
stone rise into the sky as the devil with a deep sigh emerged from the maiden and set course for Asia
carrying the huge block through the air, a clear sign of the power that Christians have over demons.
The grateful Empress wanted to shower gold on Averkios, but he would not have it. He stayed for some
time in the city, strengthening the Christians by his preaching and miracles until God commanded him
in a vision to leave for Syria. He went first of all to Antioch and thence to Apomia, where he fought
victoriously against the heresy of Marcian. He even crossed the Euphrates reaching Nisebus
and passed through the whole of Mesopotamia, pursuing the same heresy into the regions where it had
most adherents. No bishop of Averkios's day covered such vast distances as he to proclaim,
like the apostles the good news of salvation, and for this reason he received the title of
equal to the apostles. From Mesopotamia he went to Selissia, Lyconia, and Pesidia. Several years later
he returned to Frigia where his spiritual flock received him with great rejoicing. In Frigia during
some years of peace Averkios continued teaching, baptizing, healing the sick and casting out demons.
He wrote a book of spiritual direction for his clergy to guide them after his departure.
Then he went up into a high mountain to pray. There he made a hot spring,
gush forth, and having received the revelation that his death was near at hand,
he came down from the mountain into the city to prepare his tomb.
The inscription he had carved on it can still be read in a museum in Rome.
A citizen of no mean city. I made this tomb in my lifetime so as to have a resting place for my body.
My name is Averkios. I am a disciple of a pure shepherd who pastures his flock on hills and
dails, who has great all-seeing eyes. He it is who taught me the faithful scriptures of life,
who sent me to Rome to look upon the raining city and to see the queen in clothing of rot gold.
There I saw a people who are marked with a shining seal. I have seen the plains of Syria and
resplendent Nisebis. I have crossed the Euphrates with Paul as my constant companion. Faith
was everywhere my guide. Everywhere she served me a fish of great size from a most pure water spring
that was caught by a pure virgin. She has an exquisite wine that she gives with bread.
Having made these preparations, Saint Averkios gathered those near him,
bade them farewell, and raising his hands in eyes heavenward, he fell asleep to join the choir of angels.
Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
