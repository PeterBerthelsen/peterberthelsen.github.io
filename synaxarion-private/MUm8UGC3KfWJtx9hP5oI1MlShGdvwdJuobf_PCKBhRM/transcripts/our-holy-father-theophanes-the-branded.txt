In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.
The reading from the Sinaxadion, for October 11, on which we commemorate, our Holy Father
Thelphanus, the Branded, the memory of our Holy Father Thelphanus, the Branded, Grapthos,
Confessor, Hypnographer, and Bishop of Nysia, who died in peace.
Saint Thelphanus was born in Palestine in 778 of devout parents who were noted for their hospitality.
They instructed their sons in divinity and secular learning, then they sent them to the
monastery of St. Sava's to complete their education and, above all, to be trained up in the
monastic life, which is the science of sciences and art of arts.
Thelphanus' humility and obedience were as great as his elder brother Theadors, as were
his learning and gift as a Hypnographer.
They were both ordained priest and were intent on growing daily in the love of God.
When Leo the Armenian in the years 813 through 820 launched a new wave of persecution against
the venerators of the Holy Images.
He proceeded against the Orthodox bishops, first of all, and then abolished all sacred
images, ordering the destruction of icons and frescoes in the churches.
His impiety aroused the anger of God, who allowed the Arabs to invade and occupy much
of the territory of the empire, including Palestine and the monastery of St. Sava's.
The patriarch Thomas of Jerusalem decided, therefore, to send the two brothers, Theodore
and Thelphanus from St. Sava's on a mission, first to Rome, and then to Constantinople, with
the aim of convincing the emperor of his error.
Leo admired the courage and learning of the two brothers, but when he found he could not
win them over to his side, he had them tortured and exiled, forbidding anyone to aid or comfort
them under pain of death.
Mercifully, this exile did not last long, for the emperor Leo was soon assassinated and succeeded
by Michael II in the years 820 through 829.
During whose reign persecution of the Orthodox abated, although veneration of the Holy
Icons was not officially restored.
But when Theophilos, in the years 829 through 842, came to the throne after his father, persecution
began again on a wider scale, and with a hitherto unknown ferocity.
St. Theodore and St. Thelphanus were tortured anew, and, for love of the Orthodox faith,
suffered hunger, thirst, mocking, blows, prison, and exile to the island of Ephesia.
They were brought before the emperor and tortured in his presence.
During the course of four days, but their courage was indomitable.
Finally, he had the cause of their condemnation branded on their foreheads in twelve lines
of Iambic verse.
They were exiled to Apamea in Bethinia, where all wondered at their orthodoxy, their askecies,
and their perfect love.
Theodore gave up his soul to God at Apamea, for he was worn out by ill treatment and old age,
but he could not be buried for the emperor had forbidden it to either of the brothers.
Thelphanus was next banished to Thessalonica, which he illumined with his orthodox teaching
until he was recalled from exile, with the other confessors by the most pious Empress Theodore
and her son, Michael.
The patriarch of Constantinople, St. Mythodios, consecrated Thelphanus as metropolitan of Nicaea
in the year 842.
Here he spent the last years of his life in peace, tending his spiritual flock with
wisdom and composing many poetic canons and hymns that we chant to this day on the feasts
of the Lord and his saints.
Through the prayers of our Holy Father's O Lord, Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us
and save us.
